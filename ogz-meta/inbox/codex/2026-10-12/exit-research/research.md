# Exit methods and numerical candidates for OGZPrime

Prepared for Trey, 2026-10-12. **Use different exit shapes for different strategy families.** Trend-following, mean-reversion and intraday structural setups do not have the same reason to close a trade. A fixed percentage remains a valid benchmark, and may win for a particular instrument/timeframe, but public research does not establish one stop, profit target or break-even trigger that is best across this bot.

This document separates published numbers, current source behavior, and proposed experiments. **No exit settings or production code were changed.** The candidates below are not approved production values, and no profitability backtest was run for this research. The existing hold on exit, stop, break-even and trailing changes remains in place.

The useful institutional lesson is method selection and measurement. These sources do not disclose a common hedge-fund setting for “break even at 1R,” “sell half at 1R,” or “trail every trade by 0.5%.” No universal empirical justification for those exact rules was established in this research.

## Units that must stay separate

- **Price percentage:** a 1% stop is a price move relative to entry. It is not 1% of account equity.
- **ATR:** average true range in price units on a specified timeframe and period. ATR(14) on one-minute bars is different from ATR(14) on one-hour bars. For entry price P, a k-ATR initial distance corresponds to `100 × k × ATR / P` percent.
- **R:** the initial entry-to-stop distance, frozen for comparison. A 2R target is twice that distance. Realized net R also includes fees, spread and slippage. Moving the stop must not silently redefine the original R benchmark.
- **Time:** 240 minutes is 16 fifteen-minute bars but four one-hour bars. Exchange trading-session time, wall-clock time and a number of completed bars are not interchangeable across stocks and 24/7 crypto.

Example: with entry at $100, a 0.5% stop is $0.50. If ATR is $0.20, that stop is 2.5 ATR; if ATR is $2, it is only 0.25 ATR. The same percentage represents very different room for ordinary movement. This explains why a fixed percentage needs instrument/timeframe-specific evidence rather than automatic rejection or universal adoption.

A wider stop does not inherently mean more dollar risk if quantity is reduced accordingly. Under a simplified cash-instrument model, quantity is planned dollar risk divided by per-unit stop distance, before allowing for execution costs and gaps. That is comparison math, not a change to this bot's sizing policy.

## Break even, partial profits and trailing activation

**Break even should mean economic break even.** An exit at entry price still pays fees and spread/slippage. For a simple long position with fees charged on entry and exit notional, fee-only break-even sell price is `entry × (1 + entryFeeRate) / (1 − exitFeeRate)`. Hypothetical 0.20% per side means approximately **+0.4008%**, before spread/slippage. This is an illustration, not a current Kraken or Alpaca fee quote. Stop execution can be worse than its trigger.

No universal break-even trigger was established by the primary sources. For a future experiment, compare **no extra automatic break-even move** with an economic break-even move after **1.5R**, keeping the initial protective stop. If that materially helps, a neighboring **2R** test checks sensitivity. These are candidate triggers, not profit guarantees. Current separate break-even settings are percentage-based; R-based behavior needs an explicit owner/consumer implementation decision rather than a field that only looks equivalent.

For partial profits, compare **no profit scale-out** with **25% at 1.5R**, while retaining the remainder's strategy exit. Compare the current global **50% at 1R** as the existing-policy control. With no costs, selling 25% at 1.5R and the remainder at 3R produces **2.625R**, versus **3R** for holding the entire position; if the remainder instead exits at entry it produces **0.375R**. Partial profit-taking exchanges some upside for a different return path. A smaller fraction is not proven to maximize return or reduce every risk measure. The Research Affiliates study's half-liquidation on losses is not evidence for selling half of winners.

For trailing activation, an initial research comparison of **1R versus 1.5R** is more interpretable across varying initial stop distances than blindly reusing +0.5%. However, this is a proposed behavior comparison, not a currently effective strategy-specific setting. A trail should move only in the protective direction; its ATR timeframe, activation, adjustments and clamps must be stated together. Do not mix current changing ATR and frozen entry ATR without explicitly choosing which is intended.

## Costs can reverse a seemingly attractive target

Let S be loss-stop distance, T the profit target and C the equal assumed round-trip cost per completed trade, all as percentages of entry price. In a simplified two-outcome model:

`net expectancy = winProbability × T − (1 − winProbability) × S − C`

`break-even win probability = (S + C) / (S + T)`

For a 1% stop and 2% target:

| Hypothetical round-trip cost | Win rate needed for zero expectation |
| --- | --- |
| 0% | 33.33% |
| 0.10% | 36.67% |
| 0.40% | 46.67% |
| 0.80% | 60.00% |

Real exits are not just two outcomes: partial fills, trails, gaps and timeouts change the distribution. Evaluate actual average net win/loss, not only the advertised 2:1 target ratio. Costs here are illustrations, not venue fees or fixed allowances to install.

## What would decide between the candidates

Keep entries and the initial dollar-risk basis comparable, then record actual filled net PnL, maximum drawdown, losing-tail size, trade count, exposure and turnover. Split results by strategy, instrument, timeframe and session/volatility conditions; do not pool stock and crypto costs. Compare chronological held-out periods, record every tried variant, and avoid choosing a lone winning parameter surrounded by poor neighbors.

Record maximum favorable/adverse excursion: how far eventual winners moved against entry, how much profit occurred before the exit, and whether a stopped-out trade would later have hit its original target within its original holding horizon. Those observations can inform an ATR distance, break-even trigger or timeout; they do not by themselves prove the alternative policy's portfolio results. Replay the complete policy because changing exits changes available capital, overlapping positions and future entries.

Intrabar path matters. A candle touching both target and stop does not reveal which filled first. Use sufficiently granular execution data or show the unresolved outcome bounds; do not assign the favorable ordering. Broker fills, spread, slippage, quantity, partial exits and the actual session transfer must remain in the replay.

The choice I would take forward is **trend-shaped exits for trend strategies, signal/target completion for mean-reversion, and structural/time-bounded exits for intraday setups**, with fixed percentages retained as honest controls. Final numbers should follow this bot's held-out net results. The current owner mismatches above must be resolved in the existing exit work before claiming a strategy-specific candidate is active.
